Why we built this

This gallery began as a look-up list for a young girl in Riyadh who was curious about mechanical engineering, and wanted to see women already doing the work she dreamed of. It has grown into a free, worldwide collection — a living database of public role models she, and other girls, can return to as their interests widen.

The aim is simple: make success visible. When a girl can read about a minister, a founder, a lab head, or a designer whose path started somewhere ordinary, it becomes easier to imagine her own. We hope parents and teachers will use it the same way — as a conversation starter, not a ranking, and as an invitation to curiosity and ambition.

Open any card for a short mini-CV: her role, a plain-language biography, experience, achievements, and links to public sources. Return to the gallery to start looking.

How it’s built

This is a simple static website — HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — hosted for free on GitHub Pages. There is no account to create and nothing to buy.

Every profile is researched from public sources: official biographies, reputable press, and Wikipedia. Each mini-CV lists the links we used. Portraits appear only when they come from Wikimedia Commons or an official page, and only when they are clearly the right person. If we cannot find a reliable photo, the card shows her initials. Nothing here is invented — not awards, not job titles, not links, and not faces.

How it’s refreshed

Every Sunday, an AI assistant runs a weekly refresh. It re-checks the current roles of women already in the gallery, then adds about ten newly verified women from around the world, across the seven fields. The data is checked before it goes live — no duplicate entries, required facts filled in, and photo files present when a portrait is claimed. Only then is the site published. Each new profile is dated, so you can see when she joined.

If something looks wrong, or you would like to suggest someone, please open an issue on the GitHub repository. Corrections and suggestions are welcome.

Accuracy and photo credits

People change jobs, and a title on this site may lag a recent announcement. We correct what we can each week, and we only record what a public source supports. Photos from Wikimedia Commons remain credited to their original authors under their Commons licences. This is a starter gallery that grows over time — not an exhaustive list, and not a ranking.
